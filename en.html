Privacy Policy — Snout Notes

Last updated: 11 August 2026

In short

Snout Notes stores your records on your phone, and nowhere else. There is no account, no sign-up, no advertising and no tracking. The only information that leaves the device is a technical report if the app crashes.

What stays on your phone

Everything you enter is saved in a local database, in the app's private storage:

  • the records of the dogs and cats: name, breed, age, weight, sex, identification;
  • the behaviour traits and the notes that go with them;
  • health information, medication, meals, habits;
  • the contact details of owners and vets;
  • the photos, copied into the app's private storage;
  • your display preferences (theme, weight unit).

This data is never transmitted, neither to us nor to a third party. It is not backed up to any server: if you lose your phone, it is lost. Uninstalling the app deletes it permanently.

What is transmitted: crash reports

The app uses Firebase Crashlytics, a Google service, to report crashes automatically. It is the only component that communicates with the outside world.

When the app crashes, the report sent contains:

  • the technical trace of the error (the point in the code where the app stopped);
  • the device model, the Android version, the available memory and disk space, the screen orientation;
  • a randomly generated installation identifier, specific to Crashlytics. It is not an advertising identifier and it cannot be used to identify you; it serves to group several crashes coming from the same device.

The report contains no data from your records: no animal name, no owner name, no note, no photo. That is a constraint written into the app's code.

These reports are used solely to fix defects in the app. They are neither sold, nor used for advertising purposes, nor shared with third parties other than Google, which acts here as a technical subprocessor.

Google keeps crash reports for 90 days, then deletes them. See Google's privacy policy.

Reports are queued locally and sent on the next connection: the app remains fully usable offline.

Permissions

The app requests no permissions. To add a photo, it opens either your phone's camera or the system photo picker: in both cases it is the system app that runs and handles its own permissions, and it returns only the image you chose. Internet access appears on the Play listing because the crash reporting component requires it.

What the app does not do

  • No account, no sign-up, no password.
  • No advertising, no analytics tool, no tracking.
  • No access to your location, your contacts, your messages or your files.
  • No data resold.

Your data, your rights

Every record and every photo can be deleted from within the app. Uninstalling deletes all local data. Because crash reports are anonymous and carry no personal identifier, they cannot be traced back individually in order to be deleted on request; they disappear automatically after 90 days.

Children

The app is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect any data concerning them.

Changes

Any change to this policy will be published on this page, with the update date shown above.
